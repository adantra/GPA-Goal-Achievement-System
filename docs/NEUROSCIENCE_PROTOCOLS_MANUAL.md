## Overview

GPA is not a generic to-do list. Every feature is rooted in a specific finding from neuroscience or behavioral psychology. The protocols operate at three levels:

| Level | Protocols | Purpose |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Goal level** | WOOP, Pre-Mortem, Identity Framing, Goldilocks Rule | Set the right goal, anticipate failure, anchor to identity |
| **Milestone level** | Implementation Intentions, GO/NO-GO Actions | Turn intention into automatic behavior |
| **System level** | Amygdala Protocol, Space-Time Bridge, Reward Prediction Error | Maintain emotional engagement and focus |

The protocols are designed to be used in combination. A single goal might have an identity statement, a WOOP exercise, a pre-mortem, and milestones with if-then plans and GO/NO-GO actions — each reinforcing the others.

## Implementation Intentions

**Location:** On each milestone (expand a milestone to see its If-Then Plans)  
**Research:** Peter Gollwitzer (1999) — Implementation intentions nearly **double** the rate of goal follow-through compared to motivation alone.

### What It Is

An implementation intention is a pre-planned response to a specific situation. Instead of relying on willpower in the moment, you decide in advance: *"When situation X arises, I will do Y."*

### The Three Fields

| Field | Prompt | Example |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Trigger** (When) | "When..." | *When I sit at my desk after lunch* |
| **Action** (I will) | "I will..." | *I will practice piano for 20 minutes* |
| **Fallback** (If too hard) | "If [obstacle], then..." | *If I'm too tired, I'll just do 5 minutes of scales* |

The fallback field is optional but strongly recommended. It prevents all-or-nothing thinking — one of the most common reasons people abandon goals.

### How to Use It

1. Open any **milestone** on a goal card
2. Click **Edit** (pencil icon) on the milestone
3. Scroll to the **"If-Then Plans"** section
4. Click **"Add If-Then Plan"**
5. Fill in the Trigger, Action, and (optionally) Fallback
6. Save the milestone

If-Then Plans display inline on the milestone card in view mode so they stay visible as a reminder.

### Why It Works

Implementation intentions bypass the need for conscious deliberation. By linking a specific cue to a specific response, you create an automatic association in memory — similar to a habit, but for one-off behaviors. The trigger effectively "automates" your intention so you don't have to remember or decide in the moment.

### Pairs With

- **GO/NO-GO Actions** — Intentions define *when* you act; GO/NO-GO defines *what* to do and avoid.

## Mental Contrasting (WOOP)

**Location:** The **WOOP** button on each goal card's Protocols section  
**Research:** Gabriele Oettingen (2014) — WOOP is one of the most validated goal-achievement frameworks in psychology, with over 20 years of empirical support.

### What It Is

WOOP (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) is a structured mental exercise that combines positive visualization with realistic obstacle anticipation. Unlike pure positive thinking (which research shows can actually *reduce* effort), WOOP creates a mental link between your desired future and the obstacles standing in the way.

### The Four Steps

| Step | Prompt | Purpose |
|------|--------|---------|
| **W — Wish** | "What do you most wish for with this goal?" | Name your most important, achievable wish |
| **O — Outcome** | "What would the best outcome look and feel like?" | Vividly imagine success — engage emotions |
| **O — Obstacle** | "What inner obstacle stands in the way?" | Identify the *internal* barrier (habit, emotion, belief) |
| **P — Plan** | "If [obstacle], then I will..." | Create an if-then response to the obstacle |

### How to Use It

1. Open a goal card and expand its details
2. In the **Protocols** section, click the **WOOP** button
3. A guided 4-step modal appears — work through each step in order
4. You can navigate between steps using the step buttons at the top
5. Click **Complete WOOP** (or **Update WOOP** if revising) when done
6. A compact summary of your WOOP appears on the goal card

### Key Principles

- **Be specific.** "I want to be healthy" is too vague. "I want to run a 5K in under 30 minutes" is specific.
- **Focus on inner obstacles.** The obstacle step asks about what's *within you* — not external circumstances. Think: procrastination, fear, self-doubt, boredom.
- **Revisit periodically.** Your obstacles may shift as you progress. Re-run the WOOP exercise every few weeks.
- **It's OK if the answer is "don't pursue this."** Sometimes WOOP reveals that you don't actually want a goal enough. That's a valuable signal, not a failure.

### Why It Works

Pure positive fantasies about the future feel good but reduce the energy needed for action (the brain partly "pre-experiences" the reward). Mental contrasting — imagining success *and then* imagining the obstacle — creates a strong association between the desired future and the reality that stands in the way. This energizes effort and directs it at the actual barrier.

### Pairs With

- **Amygdala Protocol** — Both activate realistic threat assessment. WOOP focuses on one key inner obstacle; the Amygdala Protocol activates broader fear-based motivation.
- **Pre-Mortem** — WOOP identifies one core obstacle; Pre-Mortem maps *many* possible failures.

## Obstacle Pre-Mortem

**Location:** The **Pre-Mortem** button on each goal card's Protocols section  
**Research:** Gary Klein (1989) — Prospective hindsight increases the ability to identify reasons for future outcomes by **30%**.

### What It Is

A pre-mortem is the opposite of a post-mortem. Instead of analyzing what went wrong *after* failure, you imagine failure *before it happens* and work backward to understand why. This leverages "prospective hindsight" — imagining an event has already occurred makes it far easier to generate explanations.

### How to Use It

1. Open a goal card and expand its details
2. In the **Protocols** section, click the **Pre-Mortem** button
3. The modal prompts: *"Imagine it's 3 months from now and you've completely failed. What went wrong?"*
4. For each failure scenario:
   - Describe **what went wrong** (the failure)
   - Describe the **preventive action** (how to stop it)
5. Click **"Add to Pre-Mortem"** for each pair
6. Click **Save Pre-Mortem** when done

### The Flagging System

During reviews (weekly or whenever you revisit a goal), you can **flag** any pre-mortem item as "is happening":

- Click the **warning triangle** icon next to any item to flag it
- Flagged items turn red with a warning: *"This failure is starting to happen — activate your preventive action!"*
- A red warning banner appears on the goal card itself showing the count of flagged items
- Click **Review** on the banner to jump back into the Pre-Mortem modal

### Example

| What went wrong | Preventive action |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| *I stopped showing up after week 3 because I got bored* | *Schedule practice as a non-negotiable calendar event; vary the routine weekly* |
| *I couldn't find a practice partner and lost motivation* | *Join a community group before I start; post commitment publicly* |
| *I spent too much time on theory and never practiced* | *Set a rule: for every 10 min of theory, 30 min of practice* |

### Why It Works

When you ask "Will this succeed?" your brain tends to confirm. When you assume failure has already happened and ask "Why did it fail?", your brain engages in more creative and realistic analysis. This technique is especially powerful for overconfidence — the most common planning bias.

### Pairs With

- **WOOP** — WOOP identifies one core inner obstacle; Pre-Mortem maps the full landscape of potential failures.
- **Weekly Review** — The perfect time to revisit your pre-mortem and check if any predicted failures are starting to happen.

## Identity-Based Goal Framing

**Location:** Appears on each goal card (violet text); click to edit  
**Research:** James Clear, *Atomic Habits* (2018) — Identity-based goals produce more durable behavior change than outcome-based goals.

### What It Is

Instead of framing your goal as an outcome you want ("I want to lose weight"), you frame it as an identity you're becoming ("I'm someone who takes care of their body"). This shifts motivation from external reward to internal self-concept.

### How to Use It

1. On any goal card, you'll see either:
   - A violet identity statement (if one is set), or
   - A "Set identity statement..." button (if not yet set)
2. Click to open the editor
3. Fill in: *"The person I'm becoming: ___"*
4. Use the framing tip: Reframe "I want to [behavior]" into "I am [identity]"
5. Save

### Reframing Examples

| Outcome-based (weaker) | Identity-based (stronger) |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| I want to write a book | I am a writer |
| I want to run a marathon | I am a runner |
| I want to learn Spanish | I am someone who speaks multiple languages |
| I want to save money | I am someone who builds wealth |
| I want to eat healthy | I am someone who nourishes their body |

### Why It Works

Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become. When your behavior aligns with your self-image, it creates internal consistency pressure (cognitive dissonance works *for* you). Saying "I'm not a smoker" is far more powerful than "I'm trying to quit" — it moves the decision from willpower to identity.

### Pairs With

- **WOOP** — Your identity statement informs the "Wish" step.
- **Implementation Intentions** — "When [trigger], I will [action] because that's who I am."

## Amygdala Protocol

**Location:** Neuro-Tools sidebar > **Amygdala Protocol**  
**Research:** Inspired by amygdala-driven threat detection and Andrew Huberman's work on fear as a performance motivator.

### What It Is

The Amygdala Protocol (also called "Foreshadowing Failure") is a writing exercise that forces you to confront what happens if you *don't* pursue your goals. By vividly describing the negative consequences of inaction, you activate your brain's threat-detection system (the amygdala), creating urgency and emotional fuel.

### How to Use It

1. Open the **Amygdala Protocol** from the Neuro-Tools sidebar
2. Write at least 50 words describing what your life looks like if you fail to act on your goals
3. Use the AI tools available in the modal:
   - **Analyze** — Get scored feedback on the emotional depth of your writing
   - **Enhance** — AI strengthens your writing for greater emotional impact
   - **Generate** — AI drafts a foreshadowing based on your actual goals
4. Your writing is saved automatically and can be revisited anytime

### Why It Works

Humans are more motivated by loss aversion than potential gain (Kahneman & Tversky). The Amygdala Protocol leverages this asymmetry — by vividly imagining the *cost* of inaction, you create a powerful emotional drive that supplements your positive motivation.

## Space-Time Bridge

**Location:** Neuro-Tools sidebar > **Space-Time Bridge** (opens full-screen)  
**Research:** Based on Andrew Huberman's attentional focus protocol and meditation research on spatial awareness.

### What It Is

A guided attentional exercise that cycles through six spatial-focus phases:

| Phase | Duration | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **Internal** | 5 sec | Your breath, heartbeat, internal sensations |
| **Body** | 5 sec | The surface of your skin, contact points |
| **Near** | 5 sec | The immediate space around you |
| **Far** | 5 sec | A distant object or the horizon |
| **Broad** | 5 sec | The entire globe — bird's-eye view |
| **Return** | 5 sec | Back to your specific goal and self |

### How to Use It

1. Click **Space-Time Bridge** in the Neuro-Tools sidebar
2. A full-screen guided player opens with visual and audio cues
3. Follow the prompts through each phase
4. After the cycle, you'll feel recalibrated and focused

### Why It Works

Deliberately shifting your attentional spotlight across spatial scales "resets" your visual and cognitive system. It breaks rumination loops, reduces tunnel vision, and returns you to a state of open but directed focus — the ideal state for goal pursuit.

## GO / NO-GO Actions

**Location:** On every milestone  
**Research:** GO/NO-GO paradigm from cognitive neuroscience (Aron et al., 2004) — response inhibition is a trainable skill.

### What It Is

Every milestone in GPA requires at least one **GO** action (something to do) and one **NO-GO** action (something to avoid). This is not optional — it's enforced by the system.

### Examples

| GO (Actions to Take) | NO-GO (Actions to Avoid) |
|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Practice for 30 min every morning | Don't skip two days in a row |
| Read 20 pages before bed | Don't browse social media after 10 PM |
| Ask for feedback after each session | Don't compare yourself to experts |

### Why It Works

Most goal systems only track what to do. But knowing what to *avoid* is equally important. The GO/NO-GO paradigm engages the prefrontal cortex's inhibitory circuits, making it easier to resist counterproductive behaviors. By explicitly naming both sides, you create a complete behavioral map for each milestone.

### Pairs With

- **Implementation Intentions** — Intentions define *when* and *where*; GO/NO-GO defines the *what* and *what not*.

## Reward Prediction Error

**Location:** Automatic — triggers when you complete a milestone  
**Research:** Wolfram Schultz (1997) — Dopamine neurons encode reward prediction errors, not rewards themselves.

### What It Is

When you complete a milestone, GPA uses a **variable ratio schedule** to determine your reward. There's a ~15% chance of triggering a "JACKPOT" reward instead of the standard completion message. You never know which milestone will be the jackpot.

### Why It Works

Dopamine — the neurotransmitter most associated with motivation — doesn't respond to expected rewards. It responds to *unexpected* rewards (prediction errors). This is why slot machines are addictive: the unpredictability itself is what drives engagement. GPA applies the same principle ethically — the variable reward keeps you curious and engaged in completing milestones, because the next one *might* be the jackpot.

## The Goldilocks Rule

**Location:** Goal creation — enforced automatically  
**Research:** Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's Flow Theory (1990) — Optimal engagement occurs when task difficulty is ~4% above current ability.

### What It Is

When creating a goal, GPA requires a difficulty rating between **6 and 8** out of 10. Goals rated 1-5 (too easy) or 9-10 (too hard) are rejected with a warning:

> *"Neuro-Protocol Violation: The Goldilocks Rule. Goals must be moderately difficult (6-8) to sustain dopamine pursuit."*

### Why It Works

Goals that are too easy produce boredom. Goals that are too hard produce anxiety. The "Goldilocks Zone" — goals that are *just right* — produces the optimal level of challenge that sustains motivation and enables flow states. By enforcing this at the system level, GPA prevents you from setting yourself up for disengagement.

## Recommended Workflow

Here's how to use all the protocols together when creating and pursuing a goal:

### When Creating a Goal

1. **Set the goal** with a difficulty of 6-8 (Goldilocks Rule enforced)
2. **Write an identity statement** — "The person I'm becoming: ___"
3. **Run the WOOP exercise** — Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan
4. **Do a Pre-Mortem** — "Imagine failure. What went wrong?"
5. **Create milestones** with GO/NO-GO actions

### When Breaking Down Milestones

6. **Add Implementation Intentions** to each milestone — "When X, I will Y"
7. Include a **fallback** for each intention — "If too hard, then Z"

### Weekly Maintenance

8. **Run a Weekly Review** — check in on progress and blockers
9. **Revisit your Pre-Mortem** — flag any failures that are starting to happen
10. **Update your WOOP** if your inner obstacle has shifted
11. **Use the Amygdala Protocol** if motivation is flagging
12. **Use the Space-Time Bridge** before a focused work session

### On Completion

13. Complete milestones to trigger the **variable reward system**
14. When all milestones are done, enjoy the **completion celebration**
15. Revisit your identity statement — you've proven you *are* that person
